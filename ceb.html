Center for Excellence in Biotechnology

Harnessing biotechnology to improve livelihoods across agriculture, food, forestry and the environment in Papua New Guinea.

The Center for Excellence in Biotechnology (CEB) is one of the very few modern biotechnology research facilities in Papua New Guinea. Established in 1997 by the University Council as the Unitech Biotechnology Centre (UBC), it carries out research, training and application across agriculture and related disciplines — food science and technology, forestry, and environmental safety.

1. A Rare Modern Facility, Since 1997

The CEB was created to encompass "modern biotechnology" research, training and application in agriculture, and in related disciplines — food science and technology, forestry, and environmental safety. It therefore remains one of the very few modern biotechnology research facilities in PNG.

Three decades of building national capacity. From a departmental initiative to a council-recognized centre serving farmers, industry, students and collaborating research institutions across PNG.

2. What "Biotechnology" Really Means

"Biotechnology" is any technological application that uses biological systems, living organisms, or their derivatives to make or modify products or processes for specific use. In its traditional form — fermentation — it has been used for centuries to make bread, cheese and beer, and it underpins classic animal and plant breeding such as hybridization and selection for higher-yielding crops.

The leap to modern biotechnology is precision: researchers can now take a single gene from one plant or animal cell and insert it into another to confer a desired characteristic — for example, resistance to a specific pest or disease. It is centered on (a) in vitro nucleic acid techniques, including recombinant DNA technology and direct injection of nucleic acid into cells or organelles, or (b) fusion of cells beyond the taxonomic family that overcome natural reproductive or recombination barriers.

The potential and possibilities are wide — and the CEB has the basic facilities and expertise, in national scientists and technicians, to provide services and advice in each of these three areas. Center for Excellence in Biotechnology

3. Biodiversity · Biosafety · Food Security

These three issues impinge directly on modern biotechnology. Public awareness is the key to responsible adoption and accountability in applying agricultural biotechnology — to benefit and protect PNG's rich biodiversity, ensure bio-safety, and guarantee food security.

Why this matters

Responsible adoption is not automatic. Without informed public dialogue and stringent containment, the same tools that improve crops can also put biodiversity and food systems at risk — which is why the CEB operates to international safety standards.

4. The Objective of the CEB

The CEB strives to accomplish high-quality research and training outcomes with an entrepreneurial characteristic — emphasizing the application of biotechnology to issues in agriculture and food production, forestry and the environment in PNG.

  • Keep it modern & state-of-the-art — operated at international standard through stringent control and containment.
  • Attract quality clients — from within PNG and overseas — through high-quality services and products.
  • Train skilled manpower — through undergraduate and postgraduate students, plus short-term training attachments.

5. Location & Reach

The CEB is located within the Postgraduate Research and Training Complex of the School of Agriculture, next to the National Analysis & Testing Services Laboratory — yet it is available for research and teaching by all other schools and centres of the university. It facilitates undergraduate and postgraduate research across the biological and biochemical sciences in the Agriculture, Forestry, Applied Sciences, Mechanical Engineering and Applied Physics schools of PNGUoT.

Other collaborators include the National Agriculture Research Institute, the Binatang Research Centre, Trukai Industries Limited and the Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research.

6. Facilities & Services

The tissue culture research facility provides excellent conditions for research and technology transfer, having met the required safety standards at an international level. It is well set to store plants in vitro for conservation of plant genetic resources (PGR) and to provide the necessary quarantine measures for national and imported germplasm — and it has the capacity to mass-propagate plants for farmers and commercial stakeholders.

The molecular biology lab has facilities for DNA fingerprinting and is available to genotype indigenous crop plants and animals. It also provides a highly sensitive diagnostic toolkit — combining conventional microbiological diagnostics with DNA-based molecular and serological techniques for the diagnosis of microorganisms and pests.

International-standard containment, national-scale service. From conserving genetic resources and quarantining germplasm to genotyping and diagnosing pathogens — both labs are open to university, industry and government clients.

7. Research & Development

The CEB collaborates in applied research and consultations with several national and regional research institutions. Examples include tissue culture activities — particularly micro-propagation (solanum potato seed system with the Fresh Produce Development Agency), embryo rescue (cocoa, ornamental orchids), plant barcoding (cocoa with the Cocoa Board) and clonal propagation (eaglewood with PNGUoT; coconut clonal production with Kokonas Indastri Koporesen) — and inoculum development (eaglewood, with PNGUoT & farmers).

The CEB is also an active partner of the National Biotechnology Network, developing collaboration with Corteva (a subsidiary of Dow-DuPont) for gene discovery.

8. Products & Services

The CEB has the technical capacity to conduct sound consultancies in agriculture and related fields. Some of its emerging products include:

  1. A range of fungal inoculum for agarwood production — to support eaglewood cultivation in the country.
  2. In vitro production of potato microtubers — to support the potato seed scheme of FPDA.
