SPISARD

The South Pacific Institute for Sustainable Agriculture and Rural Development — it is the extension arm of the School of Agriculture outreaching to rural communities in PNG and the South Pacific."

The South Pacific Institute for Sustainable Agriculture and Rural Development (SPISARD) is an institute within the Papua New Guinea University of Technology (PNGUoT), established within the then Department of Agriculture in 2003 — now the School of Agriculture. It is dedicated to sustainable agriculture and rural development, operating through the Model Village approach. Its mandate spans applied research, research and development, extension, education, training, technology transfer, rural development, community participation, capacity building, and monitoring and evaluation. SPISARD is built on participatory development, farmer involvement, community ownership, learning by demonstration, real-time feedback from communities, and interdisciplinary collaboration. It operates through the School of Agriculture, draws expertise from other academic schools and service service departments of PNGUoT, and collaborates with government agencies, NGOs, research institutes, industry and international universities — growing toward a standalone institute in the future.

1. The Model Village Concept

Model villages sit across various agro-ecological zones. They are not merely demonstration farms: each serves as a research site, an extension site, a training centre, a demonstration site, a feedback mechanism and a community participation centre, run with active farmer participation. Development takes place in the farmers' own environment, allowing real-time feedback from their perspective and satisfaction.

SPISARD was established in 2003 within the then Department of Agriculture (now the School of Agriculture), and outreach began in 2004 with the founding model villages at Tumua and Situm in Morobe Province. Over roughly fifteen years the Model Village approach was implemented across the country's agro-ecological zones. The 2021–2025 plan marks a transition toward the expanded Smart Village concept (Section 4), while keeping the Model Village approach as its operational foundation, and sets SPISARD toward a future as a standalone institute.

The target is to improve and attain sustainable integrated farming system practices suitable for the subsistence and semi-subsistence farming community with minimum impact on the quality of the environment. — SPISARD strategic objective

2. Vision & Mission

To be dynamic and innovative in providing leadership and life-long learning in extension and sustainable agriculture development, positively impacting on rural communities in Papua New Guinea, Melanesia, and the South Pacific Island Countries (PICs).

The mission realises this vision through three commitments — collaboratively developing Model Villages, creating demonstration projects and learning centres, and securing farmers' direct participation in pursuit of improved farming systems, higher productivity and income, household food security and nutrition, and environmental sustainability:

  1. Collaborative Model Villages — advance the development of Model Villages and improve the skills of those involved in rural development.
  2. Demonstration projects & learning centres — create, foster and implement collaborative action and learning opportunities through demonstration projects and learning centres.
  3. Farmer participation — secure farmers' direct participation in improving their farming systems, increasing productivity and income, securing household food and nutrition, and managing the environment sustainably.

3. Mandate & Functions

SPISARD's mandate is broader than technology transfer alone: it carries equal responsibility across applied research, research and development, extension, education, training, technology transfer, rural development, community participation, capacity building, and monitoring and evaluation. Its principal functions:

  • Applied research — in sustainable agriculture and rural development.
  • Research & development — and the transfer of improved agricultural technology.
  • Extension — agricultural extension services, technical information to farmers, and appropriate extension methodologies.
  • Training — for agriculture and community development.
  • Human resource development — building human resources for agriculture and community development in PNG, Melanesia and the Pacific Island Countries.
  • Farm implements — developing and promoting appropriate farm implements.
  • Post-harvest technology — post-harvest technology and food preservation, with nutrition.
  • Market information — market, credit and agribusiness management information for farmers.
  • Technology development — appropriate technology for farm and household.
  • Advisory services — advising rural communities, government and the private sector.
  • Biodiversity — preserving and promoting agricultural diversity in crop and livestock farming.
  • Rice promotion — popularising rice cultivation for food security.
  • Aquaculture — inland fish farming and aquaculture for family nutrition.
  • Agroforestry — agroforestry and social forestry.
  • Traditional farming systems — sustaining and improving traditional farming systems and their environmental controls.
  • Policy evaluation — evaluating research, extension and development policies.

4. Outreach, Smart Village & Partnerships

Wider outreach scope

Outreach extends beyond agriculture: alongside extension it addresses education, health, livelihood skills, gender, household welfare, HIV/AIDS awareness, and resource management — pursued in an integrated manner with the Section 3 functions.

From Model Village to Smart Village

The Model Village is the operational approach used since 2004. The Smart Village is a strategic evolution introduced by the 2021–2025 plan, not a replacement: the two are distinct stages — the Model Village the operational foundation, the Smart Village the expanded framework.

The Smart Village expands the Model Village to include housing, sanitation, water, health, nutrition, welfare and livelihoods. Agriculture stays central; the expanded scope builds on the agricultural base. The two concepts are not interchangeable.

Capacity and partnerships

SPISARD does not operate alone. It belongs within PNGUoT, works through the School of Agriculture, draws expertise from other University departments, and partners internally and externally — with academic departments, government agencies, NGOs, research institutes, industry and international universities — to advance sustainable agriculture and rural development across Papua New Guinea, Melanesia and the Pacific Island Countries.

Strategic Direction & Contact

Five strategic goals shape the 2021–2025 horizon: establish the Smart Village model on campus and in rural communities; apply AKIS/RD (Agricultural Knowledge and Information Systems for Rural Development) extension, education and research; improve the productivity and sustainability of farming; facilitate welfare improvement for farmers and rural communities; and promote the equal participation of women and youth in rural and peri-urban communities. Over the period, the plan anticipates a transition toward a standalone institute with its own staff and infrastructure, under evolving governance within PNGUoT. For now, SPISARD remains within the School of Agriculture, drawing expertise from other academic schools, service departments and external partners.

SPISARD works with rural communities, government agencies, the private sector, NGOs, research institutes, industry and partner organisations to advance sustainable agriculture and rural development across Papua New Guinea, Melanesia and the Pacific Island Countries. Enquiries from aligned organisations are welcome.
