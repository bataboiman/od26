School of Agriculture · PNG University of Technology

Grow Your Future in Agriculture

Science-based degrees, from Bachelor's to PhD, that prepare graduates to lead sustainable agriculture and community development in PNG and beyond.

The School of Agriculture operates with a multifaceted approach that combines teaching, research, and extension — carrying knowledge out into communities through outreach. It is one of 13 schools at the PNG University of Technology (Unitech), and has long offered a science-based agriculture curriculum for both undergraduate and postgraduate degree programs.

At a glance: 15 faculty members — 11 with PhDs — teach across the school's undergraduate and postgraduate programs.

1. One School, Three Ways of Working

The school's work is guided by a clear vision for what its graduates should become.

"A premier agricultural school providing high-class agricultural education to empower graduates to be innovative scientists, extensionists, entrepreneurs, and policy makers for sustainable agriculture and community development."

School of Agriculture — Vision Statement

That vision shapes everything from curriculum design to farm-facility use: students are trained not only to understand agricultural science, but to apply it — as researchers, as extension workers carrying knowledge back to communities, as entrepreneurs building agribusinesses, and as policy makers shaping the sector's future in Papua New Guinea and abroad.

In short: one school, three functions. Teaching, research, and extension all work together toward sustainable agriculture and community development.

2. Experienced Staff, Real Facilities

The school's research and teaching are driven by 15 qualified and resourceful faculty members — 11 with PhDs — together with a wealth of teaching experience. A robust curriculum runs across a variety of learning management systems, supporting both classroom and distance learning.

"The availability of audio-visual, laboratory, and farm facilities aids the effective delivery of classroom teaching focused on solving national and global problems."

School of Agriculture — Programme Overview

Courses are designed to prepare students for a wide variety of agricultural-related occupations, both in Papua New Guinea and abroad.

3. Two Paths to a Bachelor's Degree

The Bachelor of Science in Agriculture (BSAG) is a 4-year program for Grade 12 school leavers, or non-school leavers with equivalent qualifications who pass the school's entry exams.

"Intake Quota: School leavers – 37, Non-School Leavers – 9, International – 4."

School of Agriculture — BSAG Intake Quota

The Bachelor of Agriculture and Rural Development (BARD) is a 2-year program with similar entry requirements to BSAG, except applicants enrol directly into the third year of the degree. BARD applicants are expected to have attained a Diploma or Post Certificate Diploma in Agriculture, along with 2–5 years or more of work experience in the public sector, private sector, or a related field.

In short: two routes to the same degree family. BSAG is a 4-year path from Grade 12; BARD is a 2-year path into Year 3 for diploma holders with relevant work experience.

4. Meeting the Entry Requirements

All school leavers must sit the STAT-P Test and meet minimum grades in the following subjects:

  • Language & Literature – B
  • Applied English – B
  • Maths B – B-
  • Maths A – C
  • Biology – B
  • Chemistry or Physics – C

Applicants are required to have completed secondary education, or its equivalent, from a recognized institution.

Not sure if you qualify?

Non-school leavers entering the Bachelor of Science in Agriculture need a Grade 12 (PNG Higher School Certificate) or equivalent, with minimum scores similar to school leavers — and entry exams are a must.

5. Advancing to Postgraduate Study

Graduates ready to go further can pursue three postgraduate qualifications in agriculture: the Master of Science (MSc), the Master of Philosophy (MPhil), and the Doctor of Philosophy (PhD).

An MSc applicant needs either a postgraduate diploma from a recognized tertiary institution, or a bachelor's degree in a field of study appropriate to the proposed advanced study. An MPhil applicant needs either a merit degree or postgraduate diploma, or a bachelor's degree of honours standard, both approved by the Postgraduate Committee.

"Proven research and working experiences of more than two years are needed to apply for a PhD."

School of Agriculture — PhD Entry Requirement

A PhD applicant must hold a Master's degree in a field related to the subject of the proposed research, in addition to that research and working experience.

In short: each level asks for more independent research. MSc and MPhil build on a bachelor's or diploma; a PhD adds a Master's plus 2+ years of research experience.

6. How to Enquire and Apply

Whichever program fits you, the process starts the same way: get in touch with the School of Agriculture to confirm your pathway before you apply.

If you're a school leaver

Check your Grade 12 results against the minimum grades above, and confirm your STAT-P Test Score meets them before applying within the school-leaver intake quota.

If you're a non-school leaver or working professional

Confirm you hold a Grade 12 (PNG HSC) or equivalent — or, for BARD, a Diploma/Post Certificate Diploma in Agriculture plus 2–5 years of relevant experience — and be ready to sit the required entry exams.

If you're pursuing postgraduate study

Gather evidence of your qualifying degree or diploma and, for PhD applicants, your research and working experience, then contact the school directly to discuss candidature.

  • Do you have a Grade 12 Certificate or equivalent, or a Diploma/Post Certificate Diploma in Agriculture?
  • Do your STAT-P or entry-exam grades meet the minimums for your intended program?
  • If applying for postgraduate study, do you hold the degree level and research experience required for MSc, MPhil, or PhD?
  • Have you contacted the school to confirm your specific pathway?
